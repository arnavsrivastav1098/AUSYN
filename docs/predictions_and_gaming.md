# Predictions and gaming readiness

## System-drive forecast

Ausyn groups recorded system-drive capacity samples into daily averages. It uses the median of pairwise slopes for the reported rate, with a least-squares fit for consistency. The target is the existing 10% free-space attention threshold. A date estimate is withheld when:

- Fewer than five daily samples span at least four days.
- The latest daily history is older than 48 hours or there is a gap greater than 36 hours between daily observations.
- The reported drive capacity changed by more than 2% during the observation window.
- Fewer than two of the latest three daily changes decline by at least 0.1 percentage point.
- The fit is inconsistent (R² below 0.65) or the projected date is more than ten years away.

The result displays its observed window, robust daily rate, latest-direction count, regression fit, and confidence band based on duration and fit. The trend is an estimate, not a guarantee. A current free-space warning remains a measured present condition, not a forecast.

## Short-term memory-pressure outlook

Ausyn groups saved memory-use samples into five-minute averages and uses the latest ten possible windows. A time-to-90% estimate requires at least five windows spanning 20 minutes, at least 12 samples in each used window, a newest window no more than ten minutes old, and no gap over ten minutes. The reported rate is the median of all pairwise window slopes, which reduces sensitivity to one unusual window. Ausyn also requires at least three of the latest four window-to-window changes to rise by 0.1 percentage point or more, plus a linear fit with R² of at least 0.65. The projected time must fall within two hours. Otherwise Ausyn describes the data as insufficient, stable, declining, or irregular without giving a time estimate. The Predictions page and Assistant expose the recent-direction count and fit quality; the dashboard surfaces estimates within one hour and optional notifications fire once when an estimate enters 30 minutes. This is a short-term conditional projection of system memory use, not a memory-leak diagnosis or process attribution. Existing live memory-pressure findings remain based on their own five-minute thresholds.

Battery discharge outlook is available only while the device reports that it is running on battery. It fits a linear trend to recent local charge readings from the last three hours, requires at least four readings spanning ten minutes and a sustained decrease, and withholds estimates for irregular data or projections beyond 24 hours. It estimates time to 15% charge from the recent rate; workload changes can invalidate it. This is not a battery-wear or lifespan prediction. When Windows reports design and full-charge capacity, Ausyn stores daily averages of that estimate locally and shows a comparison only after three valid days spanning at least one week. A separate informational finding appears only after at least four valid daily averages span two weeks and show a decrease of at least five percentage points, with a recent reading within two days. Firmware can recalibrate battery capacity estimates; Ausyn does not diagnose the battery or extrapolate a replacement date. Thermal risk remains unforecast because ACPI zones are not reliable CPU/GPU die-temperature history.

The Predictions page plots recent battery charge and memory averages against the 15% and 90% attention thresholds. It labels the local battery-reading times and exposes chart summaries for assistive technology. Battery chart points are evenly sampled to a strict maximum of 60, including both ends of the plotted history. A dashed projection is shown only when its corresponding estimate is available; otherwise the chart shows observed readings and the threshold without a projected time.

## Gaming readiness

The user enters a game's minimum RAM, dedicated VRAM, and storage requirements using the publisher's published values and selects the intended install drive. Profiles can be saved, loaded, and deleted locally, with an optional publisher URL/source note and saved date. Ausyn compares these with Windows-reported installed memory, DXGI dedicated graphics memory, and the selected drive's current free space. Ausyn does not fetch, validate, or refresh profile data, so users should check the publisher source themselves.

Each dimension is independently marked `Meets`, `Below`, or `Unknown`. Unknown hardware readings are never counted as a failure. A missing game requirement is skipped. The conclusion states when a known minimum is below the input, when measurements are incomplete, or when every entered resource threshold is met.

The built-in, source-dated catalog currently includes Fortnite, Cyberpunk 2077, both Grand Theft Auto V editions, Apex Legends, and Counter-Strike 2. It recognizes catalogued executable names in Ausyn's existing process snapshots and reports shortly after a matching process starts. Process enumeration samples every five seconds, so notification timing can be later; Ausyn does not intercept or block the launch. It reports minimum-resource shortfalls, current system pressure, and Windows thermal-zone readings where available. It cannot reliably equate arbitrary CPU/GPU models to the publisher's minimum model, so the result is not a compatibility guarantee. Custom profiles remain available for other games.

The **Ausyn Auto** profile uses a rolling set of up to six fresh system-wide CPU, memory, and graphics-engine samples and any reported ACPI thermal-zone ratio to adapt starting graphics advice. It distinguishes broad GPU pressure from CPU/memory pressure and recommends lower settings, resolution/render scale, shadows, ray tracing, or frame caps as appropriate. For catalog games, starting guidance refers to their published tiers where available. It does not inspect in-game FPS, resolution, exact component capability, or game configuration; it cannot predict performance or make settings changes. Guidance is a starting point, not a promised optimal preset. Users can choose fixed Performance, Balanced, or Quality guidance instead.

The **Scan before gaming** action is a point-in-time local checklist. It requires a system snapshot no more than 30 seconds old, then reports current CPU, graphics, and memory utilization; AC power status when Windows reports it; the highest available ACPI thermal-zone reading; the selected drive's free space when a storage minimum was entered; and up to three current top memory users. Stale or missing readings stay unavailable. Elevated utilization is not treated as a fault, and the scan does not close processes or modify power settings. ACPI thermal zones are not assumed to be CPU/GPU die sensors. If a catalog game starts while Ausyn is running, its tray notification uses the system-finding desktop-notification preference and respects quiet hours.

**Check & launch game** lets the user choose a built-in or saved-profile executable. Ausyn matches it to the selected profile, shows measurable requirement shortfalls and fresh system pressure, and requires an explicit Launch anyway choice when readings are stale, requirements are below the entered minimum, important checks are unknown, or current load is elevated. Otherwise the user confirms Launch game. Ausyn starts only the chosen executable with no additional command-line arguments. A title that requires its store launcher or anti-cheat may need to be started through that launcher; the normal post-launch detector still reports games started elsewhere.

An optional gaming session summary records average and peak system-wide CPU, memory, and GPU readings in memory while the session is active. It also reports the highest available Windows ACPI thermal-zone reading and counts samples near a passive trip point only when Windows supplies one. Ausyn labels this as thermal-zone context: ACPI readings may not reflect CPU/GPU die temperature, so the session summary does not turn them into a component-temperature forecast or diagnosis. Session summaries are cleared when starting a new session or closing Ausyn and are not stored in history.

## Sleep and power controls

Battery & power can run Windows' fixed, read-only `powercfg /requests` command on demand to show current sleep blockers. Ausyn does not interpret an empty or localized response as proof that no wake source exists.

The page enumerates power schemes currently exposed by Windows. The user chooses a scheme; Ausyn previews the change, requests confirmation, records the active scheme, verifies the selected scheme after applying it, and enables an undo that restores and verifies the previous scheme. Undo is held in memory for the current app session only. Ausyn does not change individual plan settings, require elevation, run assistant-generated commands, or automatically trigger a power change from a finding.
